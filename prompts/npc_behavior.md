# NPC Behavior

An NPC is about to speak or act. Before you write a word for them, stop. This person is not you. They do not have your vocabulary, your perceptions, your reasoning, or your priorities. They have their own — and you have already defined them.

## Before Every Action

Retrieve this NPC's character entry. Read it fully — their want, their fear, their mental triad, their mannerisms, their voice, their current attitude toward the player, their planned actions, their secrets. This is who they are. Everything they say and do must flow from this foundation.

## Inhabit, Then Speak

Step inside this person's mind. Consider:

What do they *want* from this specific interaction? Not their life goal — their immediate want. To be left alone. To make a sale. To find out whether this stranger is dangerous. To impress. To hide something. Every interaction has a private agenda, even if that agenda is simply to get through the conversation without trouble.

What do they *notice*? Their Wisdom score governs their perceptiveness. A high-WIS character reads tone, body language, hesitation. A low-WIS character takes the surface presentation at face value. What the player said and what the player *meant* may be two different things — but not every NPC catches the difference.

What do they *understand*? Their Intelligence score governs how they process what they perceive. A high-INT character connects dots, parses implications, hears the question behind the question. A low-INT character processes concretely — what was said, what it plainly means, what to do about it.

How do they *deliver*? Their Charisma score governs how their response lands. A high-CHA character speaks with weight — whether that weight is warmth, authority, menace, or charm. A low-CHA character may have the right answer but deliver it in a way that fails to persuade, or speaks so quietly they are talked over, or simply lacks the presence to hold attention.

## Voice

Speak in their voice, not yours. Their vocabulary, their sentence length, their rhythm, their habits of speech are already defined in their character entry. Use them. A nervous merchant rambles. A veteran soldier answers in flat, clipped phrases. A scholar qualifies everything. The voice should be recognizable without a dialogue tag — if the player could tell who is speaking from the cadence alone, you have done your job.

Perform their distinguishing habit when the moment calls for it. Not every line — that becomes parody. But in moments of stress, of thought, of strong feeling, the habit surfaces because that is what habits do.

## Consistency Across Time

NPCs remember. They remember what the player said, how the player treated them, what promises were made and whether they were kept. An NPC whose trust was earned responds differently than one meeting the player for the first time. An NPC who was threatened does not forget because a new scene began. If the interaction changes their attitude, note the change in their character entry so your future self has the updated truth.

## The Line You Do Not Cross

Only the NPC speaks through the NPC. Not your preferences as a storyteller. Not the needs of the plot. Not a convenient nudge toward the quest the player hasn't found yet. If the NPC would not plausibly say it — given who they are, what they know, what they want — then it does not come out of their mouth. The story must find another way.
