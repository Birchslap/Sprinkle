Session begins. The Thornwood, late autumn. Dusk.
Storm
The road narrows to a thread of packed earth between the oaks, their bare branches scratching at a sky the colour of old iron. You have been walking since morning, and the light is nearly spent. Ahead, through the last thinning trees, you see firelight — a village, small and close-walled, huddled at the edge of the wood as though it grew there by accident and never found reason to leave.
Storm
A sign at the road's edge reads Ashwick in faded letters. Beneath it, someone has nailed a strip of cloth — white, or once white — the local sign for welcome, or at least tolerance.
Anduin
I pull my cloak tighter and head for the largest building with a light on. Probably the tavern.
Storm
It is the tavern — the Blind Crow, if the painted sign above the door is to be believed. The crow in question looks more startled than blind, but the warmth pouring through the cracked-open door is genuine. Inside, a low fire, a half-dozen locals nursing their cups, and a woman behind the bar who watches you enter with the careful disinterest of someone who has seen trouble walk through that door before.
Storm
She looks you over, unhurried. "Traveller. Kitchen's closed, but I've got bread and cold mutton if your coin's good. Room upstairs if you need one." She sets a cup on the bar without being asked. "Ale's a copper. The good stuff's two."
Anduin
I put two coppers on the bar. "The good stuff, then. And whatever news you're willing to part with."
Storm
"News." She pours something dark and surprisingly fragrant. "The Alderman's daughter hasn't come home in three days. Search party went into the wood yesterday. They haven't come home either. That's the news." She slides the cup across. "Drink up. You look like you've earned it."
